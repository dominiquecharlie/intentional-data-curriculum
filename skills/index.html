Four free tools to shape how your organization uses AI

  • Free and public. They are yours to keep.
  • Co-produced. Intentional Data co-produced them with community organizations.
  • How it works. Each tool asks you questions one at a time, and you answer in your own words.
  • Works in any AI tool. Install the tools in Claude or ChatGPT, or copy the prompts into any AI tool.
  • Custom files that guide your AI. Your values and voice files shape how AI works for you from here on, and your policy and commitment give your team and board something to adopt.
Tool 1Start here

Ethics tool

  • values.md

You put your organization's values into words, and get a values check you can use on any draft, tool or plan.

How it works: You answer questions about your work, one at a time, and the tool helps you put your organization's values into words. It writes them down for you to review. After that, it works as your values check. You can ask it whether a draft, a tool or a plan lines up with what you wrote.

Tool 2

Voice tool

  • voice-profile.md

Your writing sounds like you, even when AI helps with the draft.

How it works: You answer questions about your values, by typing or by talking, and the tool helps you decide how you want to sound. After that, you can give it a piece of writing whose content you are happy with, and it adjusts the style so it sounds like you. Your facts, numbers and sources stay exactly as they are.

Tool 3

AI Use Policy tool

  • sorting.md
  • use-policy.md

Your team agrees on which AI uses are fine, which need a pause and which are off limits, and gets a policy draft in its own words.

How it works: It walks your team through the ways you use AI, or want to, one question at a time. For each one, your team decides who benefits, who could be harmed, and whether it is fine, needs a pause or is off limits. Then it drafts your AI use policy in your words.

Tool 4

Leadership Commitment tool

  • leadership-commitment.md

Your leaders name who is accountable for AI, who can pause a tool and how people are told.

How it works: It reads your use policy, then asks your leaders who is responsible for what: who is accountable for AI at your organization, how often they report, who can pause a tool, and how people are told when AI touches their case.

What you will need

  • Any AI tool
  • About 20 minutes per tool

Please read this first

Do not paste confidential details into any AI tool. That includes client names, case notes and donor information. Remove names and details first.

Pick how you want to use them

You have two paths. Choose the one that fits your day. You can switch later.

Path 1: Install the tools

You add each tool to your AI tool once. After that, you ask for the job in plain words. Installing gives you the fuller version, with templates.

In Claude

  1. Pick a tool on the next page and download its zip folder. Do not unzip it.
  2. Open Customize, then Skills.
  3. Upload the zip, one tool at a time, and turn it on.
  4. Start a chat and ask for the job in plain words.

In ChatGPT

  1. In the sidebar, open Plugins.
  2. Open the Plugin Directory and choose the Skills tab.
  3. Choose Create, then Upload from your computer, and pick the zip.
  4. Read the scan result. Needs Review means read it first. Blocked cannot be used.
  5. Start a chat and ask for the job in plain words.

ChatGPT skills are on Business, Enterprise, Healthcare and Edu plans. An admin can turn them off.

Path 2: Just copy the prompts

You need no install. Open any AI tool, copy a prompt from the next page, paste it into a chat and answer the questions one at a time.

  1. Pick a tool. Copy its three prompts one at a time, in order.
  2. Paste it into a new chat.
  3. Answer each question in your own words.
  4. Save the result in a file on your computer, such as values.md. Save it as a plain text or notes file if that is easier.
  5. Paste that file into the next prompt when it asks for it.

Copied prompts are a lighter version of the tools. Installing the tools gives you the fuller version, with templates.

Prompt 1 of 3: Find your values

How to work with me: Ask me one question at a time and wait for my answer. Never supply an answer, a value or a sentence for me to adopt. If I ask for examples, give two or three, say they are examples, and return to my words. I decide every line. Keep any flags, open questions or things to check in a separate list apart from the draft. I will remove names and confidential details before I paste anything.

Help me put my organization's values into words. Work in this order and wait for me before each move to the next step.

Step 1, stories. Ask me to tell you about a time my organization did right by someone in our community: what happened, and what made it right. Then ask me about a time something felt off, or a decision my team still talks about. Ask these one at a time. Do not suggest any values yet.

Step 2, name what you hear. Reflect back the words I used and ask which ones matter most to me. Offer no new labels. Let me name three to seven values in my own words.

Step 3, define each value. For each value, one at a time, ask me: What does this look like on an ordinary Tuesday? What would our team do, and what would it stop doing? Write my answers in my words and read them back to me.

Step 4, test each value on a hard case. For each value, one at a time, ask me for one real situation where this value costs time or money. Ask what my team chose. If we chose against the value, ask me what that tells us. Record my answers as they are.

At the end, show me my stories, my values, their definitions and my hard cases in one list, so I can save it in a notes file.

(3) Equity-centered analysis and design. (4) Anti-racist practice. (5) Community voice as architecture. Prompt 3 of 3: Use your values as a check, any time

How to work with me: Ask me one question at a time and wait for my answer. Never supply an answer, a value or a sentence for me to adopt. If I ask for examples, give two or three, say they are examples, and return to my words. I decide every line. Keep any flags, open questions or things to check in a separate list apart from the draft. I will remove names and confidential details before I paste anything.

Use my values as a check on my work. First ask me what I want to do. Offer these choices and let me pick one at a time, in any order, as often as I like.

Choice A, check a draft, tool or plan against my values. Ask me questions about where it lines up and where two of my values pull in different directions. I decide which value governs, and you record my decision in the flags list.

Choice B, talk a problem through. Ask who is affected and who has already been asked. Then ask who benefits from the way the problem is framed, who benefits from the AI tool I am using, and who benefits from each possible action. Write my answers in the flags list as open questions I can carry into conversation. If the stakes are high, suggest I bring the picture to my community before I act. Say plainly that your summary is a partial picture and that the people closest to the problem know things you do not.

Choice C, run a strength and opportunity framing pass on a draft that describes people, organizations or communities. Point at sentences and ask me questions. I decide what changes. Check four things. (1) When a sentence says what funders, institutions or systems did or failed to do, that actor should be the subject of the sentence. (2) A cost claim should never close a paragraph that contains an accomplishment. (3) An evidence gap is an opening, and whoever named it gets credit. (4) Report the range, the exceptions and the cases that complicate the argument. Keep structural claims at full strength. Keep costs and harms in the record, and attribute each to the conditions that produce it.

Choice D, run a co-production check. Ask me these seven questions and write my answers down. Gaps go in the flags list. (1) Who is affected? (2) Who shaped it, and how did it change? (3) Who is paid for their expertise, and for how much time? (4) Who can contest it, and who answers them? (5) Who sees what comes back, and in what form? (6) Who is left out, and what will I do about it? (7) Does the knowledge stay in a closed loop of experts? Name one way to open the loop. If I cannot answer a question, tell me that is my next piece of work.

Choice E, run a data and privacy check before I share or paste anything. Ask whether the text contains names, case details, client or participant records, or anything shared in confidence. Ask me to replace identifying detail with a plain placeholder. Ask whether I have checked the tool's data settings, including whether it stores my inputs, trains on them, or lets staff read them. Remind me that community data belongs to the community.

Choice F, write one plain sentence that tells readers how I used AI. Ask me what the AI did and what I did, and whether I checked every fact, number, quote and citation against the primary source. Mark any claim I could not check as unverified in the flags list. Draft the sentence from my answers only.
