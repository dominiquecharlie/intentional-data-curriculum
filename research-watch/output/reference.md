# Co-Produced AI Governance: evidence base

9 sources. Maintained by Intentional Data Research Watch. Last regenerated 2026-07-25.

## Australia&#39;s 8 AI Ethics Principles

Department of Industry, Science and Resources, Australian Government, 2019. <https://www.industry.gov.au/publications/australias-artificial-intelligence-ethics-principles>
Eight plain-language principles covering wellbeing, human-centred values, fairness, privacy, reliability, transparency, contestability, and accountability.

**Why it matters.** Its plain language makes it the scaffold participants use when sorting scenarios, without needing a legal background.

Curriculum segments: W1-S2, W3-S1, toolkit-sorting. Type: foundational. Last verified 2026-07-25.

## CARE Model and Communities in the Loop

Measure, Measure, 2026. <https://communityintheloop.org/>
A practice model treating community accountability as a structural element of governance design.

**Why it matters.** It supplies the community anchor exercise and the community impact category scored in the tool evaluation rubric.

Curriculum segments: W1-S2, W3-S1, toolkit-rubric. Type: foundational. Last verified 2026-07-25.

## The AI Governance Stack: A Framework for the Next Three Years

William Fedorich, AI Governance for Leaders, 2026. <https://williamfedorich.substack.com/p/the-ai-governance-stack-a-framework>
A five-layer governance architecture covering policy, oversight, controls, audit, and disclosure.

**Why it matters.** It is the structure behind the governance charter and the maturity assessment leadership commits to.

Curriculum segments: W2-S2, toolkit-charter. Type: foundational. Last verified 2026-07-25.

## Scenario-Based AI Literacy Scale (SAILS)

Scheibenzuber et al., British Journal of Educational Technology, 2026. <https://doi.org/10.1111/bjet.70065>
A validated instrument that measures AI literacy through eight anchored scenarios across two subscales, critical-reflective and instrumental.

**Why it matters.** It is the basis of the literacy diagnostic and the reason upskilling can be measured before and after delivery instead of surveyed.

Curriculum segments: toolkit-diagnostic, toolkit-report, W1-S1. Type: foundational. Last verified 2026-07-25.

## Interrogative Reasoning and the Problem with the Human in the Loop

Styles, Williams, Teran, and Johnson, Just Tech, Social Science Research Council, 2026. <https://just-tech.ssrc.org/articles/interrogative-reasoning-and-the-problem-with-the-human-in-the-loop/>
Reframes ethical AI as a question of power, belief, and timing, and shows why a single human reviewer fails to catch the harms a community would see.

**Why it matters.** It is the basis for moving from human in the loop to communities in the loop, and it is credited to Measure, the curriculum&#39;s partner.

Curriculum segments: W2-S2, W3-S1, toolkit-sorting. Type: foundational. Last verified 2026-07-25.

## ISO/IEC 42001, AI management systems

ISO and IEC, ISO, 2023. <https://www.iso.org/standard/42001>
The first management system standard for artificial intelligence, covering the structures an organization puts around AI use.

**Why it matters.** It gives the governance architecture options a recognised external reference an auditor or funder will know.

Curriculum segments: W2-S2, toolkit-charter. Type: standard. Last verified 2026-07-25.

## AI Risk Management Framework

National Institute of Standards and Technology, NIST, 2023. <https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework>
A voluntary risk management framework covering govern, map, measure, and manage functions. Version 1.0 is under revision.

**Why it matters.** Texas TRAIGA names it as the safe harbor standard, which makes it the reference point for organizations operating in Texas.

Curriculum segments: W2-S2, toolkit-charter. Type: standard. Last verified 2026-07-25.

## Policy on the AI Exponential, including the Advanced AI Framework

Anthropic, Anthropic, 2026. <https://www.anthropic.com/policy-on-the-ai-exponential>
Two policy proposals. The Advanced AI Framework sets out four categories of catastrophic risk: biological, cyber, loss of control, and automated research and development.

**Why it matters.** Its four risk categories are the taxonomy the governance charter uses, and it supplies the pace of change argument behind the why now framing.

Curriculum segments: W2-S1, toolkit-charter. Type: policy. Last verified 2026-07-25.

## What is California&#39;s AI safety law?

Alikhani and Kane, Brookings Institution, 2025. <https://www.brookings.edu/articles/what-is-californias-ai-safety-law/>
An analysis of California SB 53, covering transparency duties and its whistleblower protections.

**Why it matters.** SB 53 is the pattern for state-level AI regulation, and its whistleblower provisions are modelled directly in the governance charter.

Curriculum segments: W2-S1. Type: policy. Last verified 2026-07-25.
